# How we got here

*Overthought & Co., internal. Written 22 November 2026, five years to the day after we opened.*

## The sock

Mr Fezziwig lost one sock from a good pair in March 2021 and found nobody sold a single one. That was the idea: take an ordinary thing, think it through properly, and be plain about the result. He chose a name that said so. He took a small warehouse unit and started with £11,500.

He launched with three products: The Spare Sock, The Paper Aeroplane and The Emergency Sandwich. Beckside Mill made the socks, Kestrel made the aeroplanes, and Hollin Bakehouse agreed to make sandwiches only when one was ordered. The first two orders went out on 8 October. The aeroplanes were on time. The socks were seven days late.

## The first winter

We opened on 22 November 2021. Forty-one people visited that day and one bought something: Mr Baddeley, two socks. The first sandwich was ordered on 6 December. We finished the year with six orders and a loss of £368.05.

Early 2022 was slow. January and February had two orders each. The pen, bubble wrap and umbrella all came in the first year, and each took weeks to sell its first one. In July the first review came in, two stars for a sock that went through at the heel. In September the first five-star review arrived, for an aeroplane that flew further than the customer could fold one. The first Black Friday, in November 2022, took us from about one order every three days to nearly one and a half a day. 2022 ended with 98 orders and a loss of £1,998.62.

## Help arrives

2023 was the year the range grew: the Daypack in February, Ice by Post in May, the Indoor Helmet in September and the Trophy in November. In May we made our first profit, £24.21. It was small. We counted it anyway.

In September, orders went from 31 to 51 in a month and returns needed someone to answer them. **Mrs Hudson** joined on 11 September 2023. A storm in the south-west the week before Christmas stopped couriers and cut orders to about one a day for a week. We still finished December with 86 orders.

## Blip, and the rest of the team

Marketing had grown, so **Mr Toad** joined on 8 January 2024 with a craze for every new channel. Blip, the very small phone, launched in March. March 2024 had 81 orders and our best profit so far. **Phileas Fogg** joined on 25 March because orders had risen from 48 to 81 and parcels needed times. He promises them to the minute. **Mole** joined on 17 June, the month we passed 100 orders for the first time.

Money was harder. We lost money every month from April to August 2024, and a Beckside order arrived 12 days late. **Robinson Crusoe** joined on 19 August to find second suppliers. **Ebenezer Scrooge** joined on 16 September to find out where the money went. In September a film of someone unrolling pre-popped bubble wrap in silence was watched millions of times, and orders rose from 93 to 127 in the month. **Bob Cratchit** joined on 4 November to do the books, and **Hephaestus** on 16 December to start the Fix Club bench.

That December was our biggest so far. A weekend paper mentioned us for nothing and sent 1,476 visitors and 31 orders in a week. We made 331 orders and £14,239.40, and 2024 ended with a profit of £5,309.68.

## 2025

In January we ordered the first small speakers from Tidewater, and in February we paid for spring stock before we had sold it. Cash fell to £1,400.05, the lowest it has been. **Sam Weller** joined on 24 February to share the support desk. Boombox Mini launched on 3 March, and March alone had 194 orders and a profit of £892.87. **Esther Summerson** joined on 7 July, when we took 210 orders in a summer month.

At Christmas a miser in the news bought gifts for a whole family, and orders doubled for a week. The busiest days we have had, 26 orders, were 7 and 16 December. December 2025 had 411 orders, £18,831.50 and the largest refunds yet. We got through it. 2025 ended with 2,527 orders and £10,241.45 of profit.

## Where we are now

In 2026 four products launched: Pocket Jungle and Skimming Stones in June, The Starter Puddle in August and The Biscuit Tin in September. A heatwave in June sent ice and stones out the door, and the month brought £14,404.40. July had the highest refunds in a month, £970.25. In October Fezziwig wrote the Christmas plan. The Trophy sells out every December, so the batch is ordered early.

In five years we have taken 7,180 orders and £322,514.15, and made £26,256.36. Cash today is £33,554.14, the most it has ever been.

## What we know

Orders arrive in lumps: a viral film, a heatwave, a gift season. The work is mostly being ready for them. Fezziwig says that is as it should be.
